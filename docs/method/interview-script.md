# Interview Script

**Product:** Freathe · **For:** demand §8 Phase 1 · **Length:** 15 minutes · **Last updated:** 2 August 2026

> **Related:** [Demand](../product/demand.md) · [BMAD path](bmad-path.md) · [Decision log](../log/decision-log.md)

A conversation, not a study. It should feel like asking a mate about their week, because a formal frame changes the answers and adds obligations neither of us needs. Five to eight of these tell you most of it — thirty is the threshold for the numbers in demand §8, not the price of learning something.

## Before you start

- **Never say what you're building.** The moment they know, they start being helpful, and helpful answers are worthless.
- **They talk, you don't.** Target four-fifths them. Silence after an answer is the highest-yield tool here — people fill it with the true version.
- **Write nothing identifying.** No names, no recordings, no contact details, no messages kept. Notes go in a file with a number at the top, not a person.
- **18+ where you can.** If it's a friend your own age, keep it to what they did with their evening and treat it as colour rather than data — the segment is 18–26 ([D-046](../log/decision-log.md)).

## The questions

Ask in this order. Follow whatever they open up; the list is a floor, not a route.

1. **What did you do last night?** Then, only after they've finished: **how did you feel at the end of it?**
2. **When did you last make something?** Anything — a video, a spreadsheet, a drawing, a bit of code.
3. **What stopped you doing more of that?**
4. **Who notices whether you make anything or not?**
5. **Describe a time something changed for you — where you started doing something you hadn't before. What actually happened?**
6. **What phone have you got?** and **have you got a laptop?** — at the end, as small talk, which is what they are.

## The one follow-up that matters

Most people answer question 3 with **"I'm lazy"** or **"I couldn't be bothered."** That is the beginning of the answer and never the end ([D-027](../log/decision-log.md)). Ask once more, in these words or close to them:

> *What did you imagine would happen if you tried?*

**The gap between those two answers is the most valuable thing in the interview.** Write down both, verbatim, in their words. Do not paraphrase into something tidier — the tidying is where the finding gets lost.

## Coding question 5

This one decides a build ([D-054](../log/decision-log.md)). Afterwards, mark each answer as one of:

| Code | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Reaction** | Someone responded to a thing they had made or done |
| **Company** | Someone competent was there, doing it with or near them, at the start |
| **Alone** | No other person in the account at all |
| **Unclear** | Don't force it. Unclear is data |

The whole product is built on **Reaction**. Count honestly, especially when it isn't going your way.

## Afterwards, within ten minutes

Four lines per interview, while it's fresh:

1. Their words for last night's feeling — verbatim.
2. Their words for what stopped them, and their answer to the follow-up.
3. Question 5's code, and one sentence of why.
4. Phone, laptop, rough age.

## What ruins one

- **"Would you use this?"** — the answer is always yes and always means nothing.
- Explaining the idea, at any point, for any reason.
- Finishing their sentence, or offering the word they're reaching for. Wait.
- Asking about the future. *Would you*, *do you think you'd*, *how often would you* — all fiction. Only what already happened is evidence.
- Arguing with "I'm lazy". Don't correct it. Ask the follow-up and let the gap show up on its own.

## Before the first one

Write down what you expect to hear — how many will describe the evening-residue feeling unprompted, how many will say *lazy*, and how question 5 will split across the four codes. Commit that file first ([D-020](../log/decision-log.md)). The git timestamp is the only proof you didn't rationalise it afterwards, and it costs ten minutes.
