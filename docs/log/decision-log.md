Three consequences: the mirror's job is to make the verdict untenable against a record; interviews ask once more past it; and no copy may describe the user, only what they did. | Accepted |
| **D-028** | No scope, no finish line — never ask how big or by when | D-016 kept a user-set target date, which was inconsistent: D-024 had killed the projection while leaving the date that fed it. | **D-029** | Show, don't tell | A goal phrased as a feeling can't be built and can't be falsified, so goals are written as things the product *does*. | Accepted |

| **D-032** | No account until there is something worth keeping | Registration before value gates the only thing that matters. **Cost:** anonymous sessions complicate storage, analytics, and the age gate. | Accepted |
| **D-033** | Crews deferred to *maybe* | A social component at signup is a barrier for exactly the person this targets — someone avoiding being seen. **Broke a dependency:** D-025's ladder used the crew as guaranteed first responder. | Accepted — but the replacement rung one was **wrong and is corrected by D-043**: this decision's own objection applies harder to a friend than to the crew it deferred |
| **D-034** | Delete *"how much time do you have?"* — every mission is small | A time budget is a deadline in miniature, asked every session instead of once, and it demands a plan from someone who has none: a bored person doesn't have "twenty minutes", they have an evening. Duration is **observed, not declared**. | Accepted |
| **D-035** | The session has to be enjoyable in itself, not only in its aftermath | A payoff that arrives only afterwards still loses the moment-to-moment fight against something engineered to feel good instantly. Every cheap route is already banned, which makes this a design problem rather than a feature request. | Accepted — supplied no mechanism for eleven decisions; **implemented by D-045** |
| **D-036** | Never ask what they want to improve — they don't know | Every self-improvement product opens by demanding the goal they don't have, which is where they stall. Direction emerges from what they keep choosing. **Caution:** "become more successful" is the user's motivation, not a promise we make. | Accepted |
| **D-037** | Platform reopened; mobile-first web withdrawn | The missions produce real artifacts and a beginner makes almost none of those on a phone; a five-minute phone mission would have to be trivial. **Recommendation: desktop web**, mobile at most a thin later surface — the trigger being on the phone doesn't mean the work has to be. | **Closed by D-051** — which is that last clause taken seriously. The recommendation was wrong in the same sentence that contained its own correction: the trigger doesn't have to be where the work is, so the phone gets the trigger and stops being a lesser web client |
| **D-038** | Ask what they think they did, then show the record — comment on neither | Pointed at *discomfort* this mechanism was demoted as a late link; pointed at **achievement** it hits the terminal one, because the laziness verdict is a self-assessment and the cheapest way to dispute one is to set it beside the record. **Four rules:** words never numbers, since a numeric estimate invites gaming once people learn the record follows · show both and **narrate neither**, because commenting on the gap is the product describing the user · never fire against an empty record · only *after* work. | Accepted |
| **D-039** | Translate the record into words they can use — never into a claim about its value | Someone who shipped a small tool doesn't know how to say so; the missing thing is language, not worth. **Never assert value or predict how a reader takes it** — unfalsifiable, and where this category over-claims. **Never inflate.** | **Amended by D-041** |
| **D-040** | A third mission kind: **reset** — go outside, clear the table, walk away | *Clear the table* removes the reason you won't start; *go outside* is what you do after ninety minutes of staring. Framed as **conditions for making, never wellbeing** — the only thing standing between this and the tip-app genre. **Hard constraint: resets never count** toward authored hours, things made, or things shipped; padding the record with walks destroys the thing that disputes the verdict. **Anti-escape-hatch:** one per session, never a swap for a mission being avoided. | Accepted |
| **D-041** | Describe what they did in plain facts; never compose the sentence | D-039's generated personal-statement line was the product writing the user's application — D-005's failure mode in a different hat, unnoticed for a whole decision. It's also harmful: a paste-ready sentence means they never articulate it, and *"tell me about this"* then catches them with nothing. The gap this fills is **noticing**; phrasing is theirs, and it's the skill that survives into the room. | Accepted — supersedes the composed-sentence half of **D-039** |
| **D-042** | **Freedom, and consequence. Discipline is not a mechanism** | Discipline is force applied against what someone wants, and it fails the way all force fails — it needs a supply, and the supply runs out on a Tuesday. What replaces it, both halves load-bearing: **you are free to do whatever you want; you are not free of the consequences.** The first is why nothing is scheduled, owed, or streaked — a mechanic manufacturing obligation is lying to the user about their freedom, they notice, and they leave — and why the session is pleasant at all, since knowing you may walk at any second is what makes starting cheap. The second is why a record exists; freedom with no visible consequence is noise. **And the consequence shown is only ever the one they cannot already see.** The lost evening is not information — boredom is time you *feel* passing, so that consequence has already been delivered by the evening, and repeating it is scolding. Seven different days, a stranger using the thing: that's the only new fact available. Which reconciles consequence visibility with *never describe the user* — the mirror isn't half a record for being kind, it's the half that isn't already vivid. | Accepted — restates and supersedes **D-017** |
| **D-043** | The ladder is ordered by **number of recipients, not familiarity** | D-033 made rung one *one person the user already knows*, confusing *easy to reach* with *safe to fail in front of*. They run opposite here: a friend's judgment persists — they're at the table at Christmas, and a bad first thing becomes a thing about you — while a stranger's evaporates on contact. So the person the user knows was the **highest**-stakes audience, sitting at rung one. Rung one is now defined by one property, **a responder guaranteed to respond**, and the user picks who. **A stranger is the default offer.** | Accepted — amends **D-025** and the rung-one half of **D-033** |
| **D-044** | There is no search demand. **The complaint is the channel** | Demand §7 said distribution dominates and then said nothing — the largest hole in the planning, and load-bearing rather than an oversight: the obvious channel doesn't exist. Nobody searches for this, because the people furthest into the loop recognise it least as a problem, so intent-based acquisition has nothing to bid on. What they do is complain, publicly, in the chain's last-link language — and a complaint is the only reliable signal that someone is in the market. **Refused:** paid acquisition (LTV unknown, so no CAC is justifiable) and influencer content (the genre requires the promises we forbid). | Accepted |
| **D-045** | Enjoyment gets mechanisms, or D-035 was a wish | Five, all consequences of existing decisions. **The mission removes the decision, not the work** — what's aversive about starting is deciding, so a mission specific enough to begin with zero thinking has had the aversive part taken out. **The first move is mechanical** — a blank first minute is where sessions die. **Visible change under your hands** — a template only enters the library if a first session ends with something to look at. **The interface does nothing.** **Leaving is free**, because the cheapness of starting depends on it. Measured, not asserted, by unprompted continuation (`FR-068`). | Accepted — implements **D-035** |
| **D-046** | Phase 0 recruits **18+ only**. The 16–18 segment is blocked, not dropped | `FR-085` requires a named responsible adult per minor cohort and the founder is under 18, so **the founder cannot hold the role their own second segment depends on**. And `FR-080`–`FR-086` are *product* requirements, while the concierge phase collects personal data on forms with no product to gate — minors in Phase 0 would engage UK GDPR and a school's safeguarding policy with none of those safeguards attached to anything. **Cost:** closes the only zero-cost channel for the duration of the concierge test. **Unlock:** a named adult who will hold safeguarding, plus the DSL of any school involved. | Accepted — amends **D-019** |
| **D-047** | Separate the operator, the responder, and the interviewer | One person writing missions, guaranteeing the response, and running exit interviews stacks three demand characteristics on a cohort already unpaid, with participants reporting to whoever's personal reply they just received. Pre-registration stops us rationalising a number afterwards; it does nothing about one bent before it's written down. **Minimum: the exit interviewer is never the operator.** | Accepted — amends **D-018**, **D-020** |
| **D-048** | A mission's duration is **recorded and never displayed** | [Initial design](../product/initial-design.md) §3.2 kept duration off the card for a real reason — telling someone a task takes twenty minutes hands them a reason to postpone it — and then §4.3's own example of a *good* notification led with *takes 15 minutes*, while `FR-021` listed estimated duration among a mission's fields without saying which side of the line it sat on. The engine needs the number: adaptation sizes the next mission from observed session length ([D-034](decision-log.md)), and it cannot do that against nothing. The user never needs it. **Cost:** none found — this was a contradiction, not a trade | Accepted |
| **D-049** | **Nothing is scheduled, including the reminder.** An ambient surface replaces the push | `FR-050` scheduled a daily production reminder while the [BMAD guardrail](../method/bmad-path.md) rejected re-entry prompts by name — and the guardrail is the one that follows from [D-042](decision-log.md). A daily reminder is an obligation mechanic wearing a helpful hat, and `FR-051` sanitising its wording is what disguised it: the copy was fixed and the schedule was the problem. What replaces it is **present rather than insistent** — a card on the home screen, there when the phone is picked up and doing nothing when it isn't. **Pull, not push.** This is the only re-entry mechanism the principles permit, and three properties earn it: **the user places it themselves**, so its presence is a choice rather than a default the product helped itself to, and dragging it off takes two seconds with nothing asking why; it cannot interrupt an open mission (`NFR-012`) since it isn't on the screen the work happens on; and it makes returning cheap without making it owed. A notification arrives whether or not it was wanted, which is the whole difference. **Cost:** the product gives up its only active means of reaching someone who has stopped opening it. That is the intended cost, not a side effect | Accepted — retires `FR-050`, rewrites `FR-051` |
| **D-050** | **Monetisation is struck out**, and *one project to completion* is not the unit of sale | Two faults, one fatal and one merely wrong. The founder cannot hold a processor account ([D-018](decision-log.md)), so there was no price to test and §10's numbers were furniture — a range invented to fill a heading. And the unit it named was already dead: [D-028](decision-log.md) deleted completion and accepted *no completion metric* as its stated cost, so the documents proposed selling an outcome the product refuses to define. **The unit of sale is an open question, not a deferred answer.** **Cost:** nothing now; a real unknown whenever payments become possible, and better carried as a question than as a stale number | Accepted — struck from requirements §10 and **parked in [future plans](../roadmap/future-plans.md)** with candidates and their objections. One seam is kept ready by **D-053** |
| **D-051** | **Two surfaces: the phone catches the moment, the laptop does the work.** The account is asked for after the introduction | [D-037](decision-log.md) reopened the platform and recommended desktop web because beginners make almost no real artifacts on a phone. That was right about production and left the product **absent at the moment it claims to intervene** — boredom arrives on the phone, at 11pm, on the couch. The two stop competing as soon as they stop being one surface: the phone holds the trigger and one mission, the laptop is where the thing gets made. The seam between them is an account, which is exactly what [D-032](decision-log.md) refuses to demand up front — so it is asked for at the first moment it buys the user something, **after the introduction**: the first mission is finished, and there is either work that would be lost or a second device to carry it to. **Cost:** sync, two clients, and an age gate with a second door | Accepted — closes **D-037** |
| **D-052** | The phone surface is a **home-screen widget, forked from SapGlance rather than added to it** | The sibling repo already solves the parts that are expensive to discover: Glance scaffolding, tap-to-advance without opening the app, a layout derived continuously from card size rather than from buckets, offline selection with an anti-repeat window, and a `:core` module with zero Android imports that runs on a plain JVM — the same shape [repo structure](../roadmap/repo-structure.md) already demands of `record` and `ai`. **A fork, not an extension:** SapGlance declares no `INTERNET` permission and leads with that as a structural guarantee rather than a policy; Freathe needs sync, and adding the permission would spend that product's single strongest claim on a different product. **Costs, all real:** Android only · a Play listing needs an account held by an adult, the same blocker as payments, so a concierge cohort sideloads instead · and the beachhead may skew iPhone, which is now a question with a number attached rather than a preference | **Superseded by D-059** — the engineering was right and the market wasn't there. Returns only if an Android-carrying beachhead turns up |
| **D-053** | Keep **one seam for payments, and nothing else** | Money is parked, not cancelled ([future plans](../roadmap/future-plans.md)), and the expensive part of adding it later is never the provider — it is the capability checks that should have existed at fifty call sites and don't. So every capability question goes through **one gate that today answers yes to everything**, and a paid tier later changes one implementation. This does not violate *add folders when the work is real*: it is a rule about where checks live, not a feature built in anticipation, and there is no billing table, plan model, or provider integration until there is a plan. **Cost:** one indirection nobody needs yet, paid on every capability check | Accepted |
| **D-054** | **Test the ladder's premise before building the ladder** | The founder's own three life changes contain no instance of the mechanism this product is built on. A teacher who was good at English, which led to writing · going outside and stopping completely · a father showing them VS Code and the two of them making a project. **Two of the three are someone competent present at the start**, which is company rather than feedback, and the third involved no person at all. Not one is *I made a thing, a stranger responded, and it changed how I saw myself.* n=1 and the founder is not the beachhead, so this invalidates nothing — but the ladder is the one part nobody else builds ([D-025](decision-log.md)) and its premise has never been checked against a single human being. It also reframes the open question about **size**: the father showing VS Code was not a large response, it was an *early* one from someone who could actually do the thing, which is a different axis entirely. **The question to add:** describe the moment something changed for you — then count *someone reacted to what I made* against *someone was there when I started*. **Cost:** if it returns mostly the second, the differentiator changes and a phase of design goes with it. Cheaper to learn now | Accepted |
| **D-055** | **Self-response exists, and may never occupy a guaranteed slot** | As a gap-filler for when nobody replies this would be the most dangerous mechanic yet proposed — cheap, infinitely scalable, and it *feels* like it works, which is the exact profile of something that guts a product quietly. Demand §3 already kills it: confidence is belief calibrated to evidence, so remove the evidence and it is a mood. **Hard line: a self-response is never counted as an external response, never satisfies a rung's guarantee, never enters `response rate`.** When nobody responds the product says nothing rather than substituting. What survives is three mechanics, none of which is validation: **reviewing your own work cold** weeks later — [D-038](decision-log.md)'s move with your past output as the external thing · **writing what you'd want a responder to notice**, before shipping, which surfaces what you actually care about and gives the real response something to land against · and **responding to your own thing first as calibration**, which is how *"nice work!"* gets stopped in any future peer market. **Confirmed reading:** the intent was always the first of these — make someone articulate a response to their own work and the gap between their judgement and the thing itself becomes visible. That is [D-038](decision-log.md) aimed at the *work* rather than at a count, and it is arguably the better version of it | Accepted |
| **D-056** | **Enjoyment is the primary retention hypothesis; the response is second** | [D-042](decision-log.md) banned every conventional lever and left two candidates, with the response first by default rather than by argument. Reordering them has a consequence: unprompted continuation (`FR-068`) stops being supporting evidence that the work was pleasant and becomes **the leading indicator of whether anyone returns at all**. The tension this creates has to stay visible — if enjoyment is the mechanism then the product competes with entertainment, and creating cannot win on ease. The escape is that it competes on a *different* pleasure, visible change under your hands ([D-045](decision-log.md)), and that distinction is the first thing that will blur under pressure. A second framing for resets falls out of the same idea: boredom **accepted** rather than managed — *take it for a walk* — which sits closer to principle 7 than *conditions for making* does | Accepted — reorders the two candidates in [D-042](decision-log.md) |
| **D-057** | **Publish what the cohort made. Never advertise the product** | YouTube is where this problem gets *consumed* even though nobody searches for it ([D-044](decision-log.md)), and it is the only channel named so far that **compounds** — replying to complaint threads costs time linearly and stops the day the founder stops. But the genre runs on precisely the promises §3 forbids, so an advertisement in that voice either breaks the positioning or is ignored, and the format that wins there is the one we banned. What is publishable without a single forbidden claim is **the cohort's output**: someone who had made nothing, three weeks later holding a small thing a stranger used. That is a record rather than a promise, has almost no competition as a genre, and doubles as an upper rung of the ladder. **Consent per participant, never assumed.** Measured in missions started, not views. **Cost:** one good video is a week not spent on interviews, so this follows Phase 1 rather than replacing it | Accepted as a channel hypothesis |
| **D-058** | **Nobody on this project can use it.** Record that, and what has to replace dogfooding | The founder is **15** (August 2026), and §5 excludes under-16 — so the person building this is below its own minimum age, and will be for a year. Every other product gets its first thousand corrections free, from the person who made it noticing something is wrong while using it. This one gets none of them. **Two consequences.** The concierge cohort stops being a validation step and becomes **the only source of use-evidence that exists**, which raises what it is worth spending on it and makes the exit interviews the most valuable hour in the plan. And every intuition in these documents about what a session feels like is **reasoned rather than felt** — including the ones this log states most confidently. **Also a horizon:** the processor account, the Play account and the Apple account all unlock at majority, which is **2029**, not next year. Nothing may be sequenced behind that date | Accepted — a constraint to design around, not a problem to solve |
| **D-059** | **The phone surface is web. The widget is not the plan** | Direct observation: **two Android phones in an entire school.** That is evidence about 15-year-olds rather than about the 18–26 beachhead, but it is decisive about who the founder can actually *reach*, which is what Phase 0 depends on — and it confirms the risk [D-052](decision-log.md) raised against itself. So an Android widget serves a market that isn't in the room. iOS is closed from the other side: a live home-screen card needs a native app, which needs a Mac, $99/year and an adult-held Apple account — 2029 ([D-058](decision-log.md)). **What survives is the insight, not the implementation.** The property that mattered in [D-049](decision-log.md) was a surface the *user places* and can remove in two seconds, sitting where boredom arrives — and an installed web app satisfies *placed, not delivered* at zero cost, on both platforms, today. **What is lost is not small and must not be glossed:** a web icon is inert. It shows an icon, not the mission. The single strongest property of the card — the next thing already in front of you, without opening anything — **does not survive on iOS at any price this project can pay.** The SapGlance reuse argument was strong on engineering and is irrelevant if the artifact can't be Android | Accepted — supersedes **D-052**, which returns only if the interviews find an Android-carrying beachhead |

## Open risks

| Risk | Why it's load-bearing |
|---|---|
| **No channel** | The largest. Distribution decides both market models and every hypothesis about it is untested ([D-044](../log/decision-log.md)); there's no search demand to fall back on, and the one channel with real access is closed to Phase 0 ([D-046](../log/decision-log.md)). A product nobody encounters fails identically to one nobody wants, and the two are easy to confuse afterwards. |
| **Nothing brings anyone back** | Every conventional retention lever is banned on purpose. Three unproven mechanisms remain: the work being pleasant ([D-045](../log/decision-log.md)), a response arriving, and the card being on the home screen ([D-049](../log/decision-log.md)). The third is a *surface*, not a reason — it makes returning cheap and supplies no motive, so it should be read as removing an excuse rather than answering the question. The hypothesis is still that **the response is the retention mechanism, not the product**. |
| **The beachhead may be on iPhone** | The phone surface is Android-only ([D-052](../log/decision-log.md)) and iOS costs a Mac, $99/year, and an adult-held account — the same blocker as payments, twice. If 18–26 English-speaking skews iPhone, the surface that catches the moment reaches a minority of the market it was built for. **Cheap to find out:** ask device in the problem interviews. One extra question, and it decides whether the widget is the plan or a demo. |
| **The widget becomes a scoreboard** | A card that is always on screen is one number away from being a streak. Whatever it shows is seen dozens of times a day without being chosen, which is the exact condition under which a count stops being a record and starts being a demand. `FR-054` holds the line — one mission, nothing cumulative — and it is the line most likely to be argued away by a reasonable-sounding request. |
| **The mood delta is the weakest number we have** | A tap on a session the user chose to start, in a hand-run cohort, run by the person who wrote the missions. Selection, demand characteristics, and the mere fact of finishing all push it positive independently of the product. |
| **Reclaimed time is felt but not bought** | The time framing scores lower on willingness to pay than the portfolio framing, and higher on frequency and open space. Both are guesses. |
| **The first ship lands in silence** | The audience ladder is the mitigation and it's unproven. If guaranteed responses feel manufactured, they won't update anyone's self-concept. |
| **How big must the first response be** | There's a threshold below which evidence doesn't change how someone sees themselves, and nobody knows where it sits. The founder's own examples suggest **size may be the wrong axis** — early, and from someone competent, beat large ([D-054](../log/decision-log.md)). |
| **The change moment may not be a response at all** | The ladder assumes what changes a person is a reaction to something they finished. The only three first-hand accounts available say otherwise: two are *someone good at the thing, present at the start* ([D-054](../log/decision-log.md)). If thirty interviews agree, the differentiator is company rather than feedback, and a phase of design is aimed at the wrong end of the experience. |
